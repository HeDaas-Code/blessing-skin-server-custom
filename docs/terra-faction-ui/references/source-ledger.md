## Early CSS accent observations

The public `cvintro` stylesheet directly exposed these section accents: Penguin Logistics `#0048ff`, Rhine Lab `#d2e915`, Lungmen `#dd1c1c`, Blacksteel `#3f1bc6`, Ursus Student Group `#ff004b`, and Kjerag `#00dab9`.

Treat these as one early campaign's direct observations, not timeless official tokens. Later event evidence shows that composition, material, and institutional motifs are more stable than a single accent.

## Confidence promotions

- `penguin-logistics-street`: `Low → Medium`. `AK-CV-01` supplies the early blue faction cue; `AK-PENGUIN-02` adds an official faction-owned event/PV with repeated night-city, hard white information, and movement framing; `AK-PENGUIN-03` confirms the safehouse and dispatch context. The result supports an evidence-bounded interface grammar, not official tokens.
- `blacksteel-contract`: `Low → Medium`. `AK-CV-01` supplies the early violet ownership cue; `AK-BLACKSTEEL-02` adds a later official Blacksteel event surface with repeated safety-yellow/black direction, industrial apertures, and equipment framing; `AK-BLACKSTEEL-03` confirms the safehouse and contract/mission context. Safety yellow owns readiness while violet is demoted to institutional identity.

## Derived conclusions

1. Stable faction identity is better expressed through information ownership, composition, material, and institutional behavior than one fixed color.
2. Modern event evidence can supersede an early faction-page accent.
3. Technical credibility comes from truthful state and restrained metadata, not invented HUD noise.
4. Official custom fonts require licensed substitutes for redistribution.
