# Blacksteel contract readiness

Key: `blacksteel-contract`

## Contract

```text
faction: blacksteel-contract
confidence: medium
default depth: moderate
task owner: protection assignment, contract scope, equipment, or deployment readiness
tone lane: neutral-dark-industrial-hazard
evidence pattern: gunmetal rack + safety-yellow direction + quiet violet owner edge
primary action: release the selected assignment after named prerequisites close
```

This is an evidence-bounded clean-room grammar, not an official token set. `complex` and `maximal` are allowed when people, equipment, route, scope, and prerequisite data are real and non-duplicated.

## Evidence boundary

- `AK-CV-01` directly exposes an early violet Blacksteel section and hard commercial-military framing.
- `AK-BLACKSTEEL-02`, the official `Come Catastrophes or Wakes` surface, repeatedly uses gunmetal/black fields, safety-yellow directional bands, industrial apertures, equipment compartments, and restrained violet ownership.
- `AK-BLACKSTEEL-03` confirms the Blacksteel safehouse and mission/equipment context in the official rerun, but reuses campaign art and must not be counted as a new palette sample.
- `AK-BLACKSTEEL-04` identifies the official Blacksteel clue as weapon data, directly reinforcing equipment/data ownership without licensing weapon imagery as decoration.
- Promote assignment, protected equipment, scope, readiness, shutter, and directional-lock relationships. Do not promote firearms, character silhouettes, campaign lettering, or generic official-site chrome.

## Visual grammar

- Field: neutral gunmetal, carbon black, or very dark violet-black.
- Signal: safety yellow owns readiness progress, verified equipment, directional release, and commitment.
- Institutional accent: violet is a narrow owner rail, assignment identifier, or contract edge; it must not carry every active state.
- Critical: red only for an unresolved danger or prerequisite that blocks release.
- Materials: hard equipment bays, shutter slats, protected cases, contract plates, recessed rails, and bounded hazard bands.
- Type: condensed operational labels with exact nouns and verbs. Avoid pseudo-tactical codes.

## Composition and information

- Structural owner: a protected equipment rack with an assignment lintel.
- Navigation: a horizontal assignment rail across the rack head.
- Primary instrument: a readiness lock relating people, equipment, route, scope, and unresolved prerequisites.
- Secondary instrument: a contract scope plate naming what protection covers, the valid window, and the release condition.
- Decision artifact: an industrial lock spanning the rack exit; it stays disabled until every visible prerequisite is satisfied.
- At maximal depth, coordinate bay shutters with the readiness scan and scope boundary. Do not add weapon diagrams.

## Interaction and motion

- Primary control: hard plate with two opposed industrial chamfers.
- Binary control: armored checkbox with a clipped corner and safety-yellow verified fill.
- Continuous control: thick segmented readiness rail for a real threshold.
- Hover/press: boundary rails move inward; press settles down into the rack.
- Focus: high-contrast double lock edge following the chamfered silhouette, with violet outside and safety yellow inside when appropriate.
- Arrival: industrial shutter reveal; commitment closes the lock once and leaves a stable release state.

## Avoid

- All-violet active states, generic military HUD grids, fake tactical codes, crosshairs, decorative weapon silhouettes, or Rhodes Island system chrome.
- Treating campaign safety yellow as an unrestricted brand color; it belongs to readiness, direction, verification, and commitment.
- Reusing Penguin Logistics' ticket stack or Rhine Lab's diagnostic chamber.

## Grayscale identifiers

The interface must remain identifiable through at least two of:

1. protected equipment bays with closing shutters;
2. horizontal assignment lintel;
3. spanning readiness lock with opposed chamfers;
4. contract scope plate attached to the rack exit.
